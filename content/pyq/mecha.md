# UNIT I: ENGINEERING MECHANICS

## 1. Syllabus Topics
* **Basic Concepts:** Review of laws of motion (Newton's 1st, 2nd, and 3rd laws), principle of transmissibility of forces, transfer of force to parallel position, resultant of planar/coplanar force systems, parallelogram law of forces, triangle and polygon laws of forces[cite: 1, 45, 46, 48, 50].
* **Equilibrium:** Free Body Diagrams (FBD), conditions of equilibrium of a rigid body in two dimensions, moment of a force, Varignon's theorem, moment of inertia[cite: 1, 43, 45, 48].
* **Friction:** Law of Coulomb friction, static and dynamic friction, coefficient of friction ($\mu$), angle of friction, angle of repose, ladder friction, and block wedge friction[cite: 1, 42, 44, 46, 48, 49, 50].
* **Trusses:** Introduction to trusses, perfect, deficient, and redundant trusses, determination of forces in simple truss members using Method of Joints and Method of Sections[cite: 1, 42, 43, 45, 46, 47, 48, 49, 50].

## 3. Predicted Must-Do Exam Topics
1. **Truss Analysis using Method of Joints:** Step-by-step joint equilibrium calculations ($\sum F_x = 0, \sum F_y = 0$) tabulating member forces (Tension vs. Compression)[cite: 42, 45, 46, 47, 48, 49, 50].
2. **Ladder Friction Impending Slip Derivation:** Moments about the base/wall to calculate minimum inclination angle $\tan\theta = \frac{1 - \mu_w \mu_f}{2\mu_f}$ or with smooth wall $\tan\theta = \frac{1}{2\mu}$[cite: 42, 44, 47, 48].

4. **Conditions of Rigid Body Equilibrium & Free Body Diagrams:** Free body isolation and support reaction computation (roller vs. pinned)[cite: 43, 46, 48].

# UNIT II: ENGINEERING MATERIALS & STRESS/STRAIN

## 1. Syllabus Topics
* **Engineering Materials:** Classification of engineering materials (ferrous, non-ferrous, polymers, ceramics, composites), carbon steels (low, medium, high carbon), alloy steels, cast iron (gray, white, ductile, malleable), and industrial applications[cite: 1, 46, 48].
* **Mechanical Properties:** Strength, elasticity, plasticity, hardness, toughness, ductility, brittleness, malleability, resilience, and fatigue[cite: 1, 42, 43, 45, 46, 48, 50].
* **Simple Stress and Strain:** Concept of normal and shear stress, linear and lateral strain, Hooke's law, Poisson's ratio ($\mu$ or $\nu$)[cite: 1, 46, 48, 50].
* **Stress-Strain Relationships:** Stress-strain diagram for ductile (mild steel) and brittle (cast iron, concrete) materials, proportional limit, elastic limit, yield points (upper/lower), ultimate tensile strength (UTS), and breaking point[cite: 1, 43, 45, 46, 48, 49, 50].
* **Elastic Constants:** Modulus of Elasticity ($E$), Modulus of Rigidity ($G$), Bulk Modulus ($K$), and their inter-relationships ($E = 2G(1+\mu)$, $E = 3K(1-2\mu)$, $E = \frac{9KG}{3K+G}$)[cite: 1, 45, 48, 50].
* **Axial Deformations & Beams:** Statically determinate stepped bars, elongation under axial loads, composite sections, introduction to bending stress, derivation of flexure formula ($\frac{M}{I} = \frac{\sigma}{y} = \frac{E}{R}$), pure torsion equation ($\frac{T}{J} = \frac{\tau}{R} = \frac{G\theta}{L}$), Shear Force Diagram (SFD), and Bending Moment Diagram (BMD)[cite: 1, 42, 43, 44, 47, 48, 49, 50].
